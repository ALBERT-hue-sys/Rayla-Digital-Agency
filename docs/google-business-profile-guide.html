Can hello@rayladigitalagency.com be the account, or do we need to make one?

Yes, it can — and it is the right choice. You do not need a Gmail address.

A Google account and a Gmail inbox are two different things, and people conflate them constantly. Google will happily create an account on an email address you already own somewhere else, which is exactly what hello@rayladigitalagency.com is: a Zoho mailbox that Rayla controls.

How to do it

  1. Go to accounts.google.com/signup.
  2. Fill in the name, then on the username step choose “Use my current email address instead”. It is a small link under the username box and it is easy to miss.
  3. Type hello@rayladigitalagency.com.
  4. Google emails a six-digit code to that address. Open the Zoho inbox, read it, type it in.

From then on, the sign-in username is the full address. No Gmail inbox is created, nothing about Zoho changes, and the enquiries from the website keep arriving exactly where they arrive now.

The one thing to check first

Somebody has to be able to open that mailbox to read the verification code. The website's forms deliver into it, so it certainly receives mail — but receiving is not the same as being able to log in. Before setup day, sign in at mail.zoho.com with that address and confirm you get an inbox. If it turns out hello@ only forwards to somewhere else, the code will land in that other inbox instead, which is fine as long as you know where to look.

If you cannot get into it

Then make a Gmail — something plain like rayla.digital.agency@gmail.com — and treat it as company property rather than anybody's personal account. It is second best, not a disaster. Write it on the record page at the back of this guide either way.

Whichever account you use

Switch on 2-Step Verification and set a recovery phone and recovery email before you start the profile. Losing this account means losing the profile, and Google's recovery runs through the account, not through the business listing. If Google tells you the address already has an account on it, someone has signed up with it before — use “Forgot password” to get back in rather than creating a second one.

Watch out. Whoever is signed in when the profile is claimed becomes its Primary Owner, and that person can remove everyone else. This is the whole reason you are doing the setup rather than anyone helping with the marketing.

Rayla is already on Maps, but there is no “claim this business”. What now?

Nothing is wrong. That panel is Google's view of an unclaimed listing, and you claim it from the Business Profile side rather than from Maps.

Look at what the panel is offering you: Suggest an edit, Add missing information, Add hours, Add website, Add a photo. Those are the things Google shows to a member of the public looking at a listing nobody owns. Google built RAYLA DIGITAL itself from information it gathered — the name, the phone number and the category Marketing agency are already sitting there — and it is waiting for someone to take it. The claim link genuinely is missing from that desktop panel much of the time; it is not a sign that the listing is taken.

Three ways in, best first

  1. business.google.com/add — signed in on the account that will own the profile. Start typing Rayla Digital. The existing listing appears in the dropdown below the box. Click the existing listing, not the “create a business with this name” option underneath it. That starts the claim, with the details already filled in.
  2. The Google Maps app on your phone. Open the listing, scroll all the way past the photos and details to the bottom, and Claim this business / Own this business? is down there. The phone app shows it far more reliably than the desktop site.
  3. Sign in first, then search. If you are browsing signed out, or signed in as a personal account, Google sometimes hides the link entirely.

If Google says someone has already claimed it

Choose Request access. Google emails the current owner and gives them three to seven days to respond. If they do not respond, you can take it. While you wait, do nothing else — above all, do not create a second listing.

Two things to fix, but not yet

The listing reads RAYLA DIGITAL, not Rayla Digital Agency, and the map pin sits well north of Kampala rather than where the business actually is. Both need correcting. Both are also exactly the kind of edit that restarts verification if you make it too early, so leave them until you are verified and settled, then change one thing, wait a week, change the next.

Watch out. If claiming feels awkward, the temptation is to create a fresh profile instead. Don't. Duplicate listings are the single most common reason a new profile is suspended, and untangling two of them takes weeks of appeals.

Online retail, local store or service business — which is Rayla?

Service business.

The three options describe how a customer gets what they are buying, not what industry you are in.

  • Online retail means you sell products through a website and ship them out. Rayla sells work, not stock.
  • Local store means customers travel to a premises and buy there — a shop, a salon, a restaurant. Nobody walks into an agency to buy something off a shelf.
  • Service business means you deliver at the client's place, or remotely. That is Rayla exactly: shoots on location, strategy in meetings and calls, everything else handed over digitally.

What the choice actually changes

Picking service business tells Google to stop treating your street address as a shopfront. It lets you hide the address, it opens up the service-areas field, and the profile still appears in local Kampala results. Choosing local store instead would publish the address and put opening hours front and centre.

The one exception

If Rayla has an office with signage where clients are genuinely welcome to turn up, you can have both: a visitable address and service areas. Only take that route if somebody is reliably there during the hours you publish. An office that is locked when a client arrives earns a one-star review that outlives the inconvenience.

Our category is digital marketing, right?

Close. The category to use is Marketing agency — which is what Google has already assigned to the listing, so keeping it is one less change to make.

The primary category is the most powerful field on the entire profile. It decides which searches Rayla is eligible to appear in at all, and there is only one of it. Marketing agency is the broadest honest fit for the mix of work Rayla does, it is already on the listing, and the two obvious alternatives are narrower: Advertising agency leans towards paid media buying, and Internet marketing service leaves out the production and brand work.

Then the additional categories

Google allows up to nine more. You can only choose from Google's own list, and that list changes, so type the words and take whatever the dropdown offers. Against Rayla's six services:

  • Digital Marketing → Internet marketing service
  • Digital Content Production → Video production service
  • Graphic Design → Graphic designer
  • Brand Consulting → try Marketing consultant, or Business management consultant
  • Social Media Management → try Social media agency; if Google does not offer it, Internet marketing service already covers the ground
  • Content Strategy → usually has no category of its own. It belongs in Services, not Categories

Add four to six, not nine

Every category is a promise. It brings enquiries for that exact thing, and an enquiry you have to turn down sends somebody straight back to the search results — which Google notices and reads as a bad match. Add what you would happily be hired for tomorrow, and add more later once the performance report tells you what people are actually searching.

Not the same thing. Categories are Google's fixed list. Services is a separate section further down where you write your own names in your own words — Social Media Management, Content Strategy, Brand Storytelling, Campaign Management, Event Management — with a line of description each. That is where Rayla's language belongs.

Where do you serve your customers — what goes here, and how many?

Fill it in, and list five. Kampala, Wakiso, Entebbe, Mukono, Jinja.

The field is optional, but for a service business it is worth filling because it is the only place a stranger learns whether Rayla will travel to them. Type a town or a district and pick from Google's suggestions; it will not accept free text.

Why five and not twenty

Google allows up to twenty, and the instinct is to use all of them. It does not work, because service areas are not a ranking lever. Google ranks a service business mainly on where its pin sits relative to whoever is searching, so listing twenty districts will not put Rayla into twenty sets of results. What it will do is make the profile read as unfocused — a small Kampala agency claiming to cover the whole country persuades nobody, and Google's own guidance is to stay within roughly a two-hour drive of where the business actually operates.

The five, and why

  • Kampala — the base, and where most clients are.
  • Wakiso District — covers Nansana, Kira and most of the ring around the city.
  • Entebbe — the airport corridor, and a genuinely separate search.
  • Mukono — the eastern edge of the commuter belt.
  • Jinja — far enough to be worth saying, close enough to be true.

Add Nansana or Kira as well if you would really drive there for a half-day shoot. Leave them off if you would not.

What not to put here

Not Nairobi, not Kigali, not “East Africa”, even though remote work for clients there is real and the website says so. Service areas are about where you will physically travel, and Google checks whether the claim is plausible. The website's FAQ is the right place for the remote answer, and it already handles it.

This is one of the safe fields: you can change the areas at any time afterwards without triggering a fresh review. So five now, adjusted later, is exactly the right way to treat it.

For contact details we put our number and the website link?

Yes — one phone number and the website, written exactly the way they are written everywhere else Rayla appears. But the number needs settling first.

Two different phone numbers are in circulation

The website publishes +256 764 065 441. The Google listing in the screenshot shows 0746 065441, which is +256 746 065 441. The middle digits are swapped, so one of the two is wrong.

Work out which one is the real number before setup day, then make the profile, the website, the WhatsApp business account and every social bio say the identical thing. This is not housekeeping: Google compares the phone number across everywhere it finds Rayla, and a business that appears under two numbers is a business it trusts less and ranks lower. There is no error message for this — it just quietly costs you.

The phone field

Enter it in international format: +256 7XX XXX XXX. Use a number a person actually answers during the hours you are about to publish, and have that phone in your hand during setup — if Google offers verification by call or text, the code arrives on it.

The website field

Type https://www.rayladigitalagency.com — with the https:// and with the www, because that is the canonical form the site itself declares. Not the bare domain, not a shortened link, not a Linktree. If the two forms disagree, Google has to guess which page is the real one.

Worth adding while you are there

Google offers an appointment or booking link field. Point it at the discovery-call form on the website. It turns the profile from a phone number into something a client can act on at eleven at night, which is when a good many of these decisions get made.

And the mailing address to verify — can she verify later if there is no address?

Yes. If Rayla has a real address, enter it and hide it. If there is none yet, you can carry on and verify by phone, email or video instead.

The first thing to understand is that this address field is for Google, not for customers. Google asks a service business for it to confirm the business is where it says it is, and to have somewhere to post a verification postcard if it comes to that. Immediately afterwards Google asks whether you want to show the address to customers. Answer no. It then stays hidden, and the public sees only the service areas.

If you have an address today

Use it — the office, or wherever the work genuinely happens from. Enter it in full, then drag the map pin onto the actual doorway, because Google's automatic pin regularly lands on the wrong side of the road and that pin is what a client's navigation follows. Then hide it. This is the best case: the address is private, and postcard verification stays available as a fallback if the video route goes wrong.

If you have no address yet

Google lets a service business continue without one. Verification then goes by phone, email or video rather than by post — which is no bad thing, since postcards to Kampala addresses are slow and frequently never arrive. Google will ask again later, and you can add the address whenever there is one.

What “verify later” really means

You can finish the whole profile and leave verification for another day, and as a schedule that is fine. But understand what you are choosing: nothing on an unverified profile is public. The photos, the hours, the description and the services all sit in a draft that only you can see, and the listing on Maps stays the unclaimed one anybody can edit. Treat “later” as a few days, not a few months.

Watch out. Never use a coworking desk, a virtual office, a mailbox service or a PO box, and never borrow a friend's address. Google looks for these and removes the profiles that use them, usually without warning. And do not type a placeholder meaning to correct it afterwards — changing the address after verification triggers a fresh review, and sometimes a suspension.

Why this is worth an hour of your time

Read this part once. The rest of the guide you can work through with the laptop open.

When somebody in Kampala searches marketing agency near me, video production Kampala, or simply Rayla, Google shows a map with three businesses on it before it shows a single website. A Google Business Profile is how Rayla gets into that list. It is free, it takes about an hour, and nothing else in local marketing returns as much for as little.

It also decides what people see when they search for Rayla by name. Right now that space is empty, or worse, filled in by Google from scraped data that nobody at Rayla controls. Once the profile is claimed and verified, you own it: the photos, the hours, the description, the phone number people tap.

What the profile actually gives you

  • A place in the map results for searches Rayla currently cannot appear in at all.
  • A panel beside search results with photos, hours, services, and a call button.
  • Reviews. Google displays them itself, which is why they carry more weight with a stranger than testimonials on your own site.
  • Numbers. How many people found you, what they searched to get there, how many called, how many asked for directions. That last one is quietly the most useful marketing data Rayla will get for free.
  • Control. Anyone can suggest an edit to an unclaimed listing. Verified owners approve or reject those edits.

One thing to settle before you touch anything

Whoever is signed in to Google when the profile is created becomes its Primary Owner. That person can remove everybody else; nobody can remove them.

This is why you are the one doing the setup. Create it on an account that belongs to the business, and add anyone who helps with the marketing as a Manager afterwards. Managers can post, reply to reviews, and edit the listing. They cannot take it with them.

For the recorded video you film one continuous take with no cuts and no editing, showing, in this order:

The profile gives you a short review link. Send it to clients you have finished work with, one at a time, in a message that mentions what you did for them. Five genuine reviews in the first month is a good target.

Where this goes wrong

Every one of these is common, and every one of them costs more to undo than to avoid.

Creating the profile on a personal Gmail

The profile belongs to a person, not the business. When that person changes phone, leaves, or forgets the password, Rayla loses the listing and has to fight Google to get it back.

Instead: create it on a business account with recovery details set, before step 1.

Putting keywords in the business name

The fastest route to suspension there is. It is also the most reported violation, because competitors report it.

Instead: the real name only. Keywords belong in categories, services and the description.

Leaving the map pin where Google dropped it

Clients follow the pin, not the address text. A pin on the wrong plot produces phone calls from people standing outside somebody else's gate.

Instead: drag it to the doorway yourself, then test it by asking for directions from somewhere across town.

Using a coworking space, virtual office or PO box as the address

Google treats these as fake locations and removes the profile, usually without warning.

Instead: use a real premises, or hide the address and run it as a service-area business.

Publishing a home address you did not want public

Once it is indexed it spreads to other directories, and pulling it back is slow.

Instead: decide at step 4, before you type it, and use service areas if there is any doubt.

Picking a vague primary category

The profile quietly never appears for the searches that matter, and there is no error message to tell you.

Instead: choose the most specific category that is genuinely true, then review it after a month against the search terms in the performance report.

Rewriting the name, address or category days after verification

Large edits immediately after approval trigger a fresh review, and sometimes a suspension.

Instead: get the details right before submitting. If something must change, wait a week or two and change one thing at a time.

Different phone numbers or spellings across the website and social accounts

Google cannot confirm the business is one business, so it trusts the profile less and ranks it lower.

Instead: write the name, address and phone number once, then copy that exact text everywhere.

Setting it up, then never touching it again

Profiles with no recent posts, photos or review replies slide down the results while active competitors climb past.

Instead: fifteen minutes a week, on the same day each week. See the next page.

Buying reviews, or asking staff and family to post them

They are detected and deleted, and repeat attempts can cost the profile permanently. It is the one mistake here that is not recoverable.

Instead: ask real clients directly, personally, and soon after the work lands well.

If the profile is suspended anyway

Do not create a replacement, and do not delete anything. Read the notice for the reason, fix that specific thing, and submit the reinstatement appeal form Google links from the notice. Appeals usually resolve within a couple of weeks. A second profile turns a fixable suspension into a permanent one.

The first thirty days

Setup is the easy half. This is the half that produces enquiries.

Week one

Verify. Fill in every field the profile offers, including the ones that feel minor. Upload ten photos. Add managers. Post your own questions and answer them.

Week two

Send the review link to five past clients, individually, each with a sentence about the work you did together. Reply to every review that arrives.

Week three

Publish the first post: a project, a new service, a hire, a behind-the-scenes shot from a shoot. Posts expire after about a week, which is the whole point of them being a habit.

Week four

Open the performance report. Look at calls, direction requests, website clicks, and especially the search terms people used to find Rayla. Those terms tell you which categories and services to add next.

After that

One post a week. Two or three photos a month. A reply to every review and question. Updated hours before every public holiday. Fifteen minutes, same day each week.

Three numbers worth watching

Direction requests tell you people intend to turn up in person. Calls from the profile tell you the listing is doing sales work the website is not. The search terms report tells you, in your clients' own words, what Rayla is being hired for — which is worth more than any of it.

Compare them month to month, not day to day.
